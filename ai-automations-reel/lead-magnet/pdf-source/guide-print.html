The 4-Task Workflow

How to automate enquiries, follow-ups, documents and team updates, with a person approving every step that matters.

The four automations at a glance

Each automation runs on its own. They can share the same contacts and data, but none depends on another, so start with the task that costs you the most time.

What makes it reliable

Three rules that keep it working

Connected tools

Your inbox, CRM, documents and chat share the same data, so nothing is typed twice.

Clear rules

Every automation states when it runs, what it does and when it stops.

Human review

A person approves messages, payments and anything unusual. Every step is logged.

Automation 01

Capture enquiries

Every enquiry is recorded and answered quickly, in your tone, without anyone copying details by hand.

Trigger
A new message from your website form, email inbox, Instagram DM or WhatsApp Business.
Steps
  1. Read the message and pull out the name, contact details and request.
  2. Create or update the contact in your CRM and tag where it came from.
  3. Draft a reply in your tone, using your price list or FAQ.
  4. Send the draft to you, and send it to the customer only after you approve.
Tools

Automation: Make, Zapier or n8n

CRM: HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive or a Google Sheet

Email and AI: Gmail or Outlook, with ChatGPT or Claude

Human approval: every reply stays a draft until you approve it. Nothing reaches a customer without you.
Automation 02

Follow up on time

No lead or quote is forgotten, and follow-ups go out on a rhythm you choose.

Trigger
A lead hasn’t replied, or a quote is still open, after the days you set (for example day 1, 3 and 7).
Steps
  1. Check the CRM each morning for leads that are due.
  2. Remind you, or whoever owns the lead, with the context in one message.
  3. Send a follow-up from your pre-approved templates.
  4. Mark the lead “needs attention” if there is still no reply after the last step.
Tools

Sequences: your CRM’s built-in follow-ups, or Make, Zapier or n8n

Channels: WhatsApp Business or email

Reminders: Slack, Microsoft Teams or WhatsApp

Human approval: only messages you approved in advance go out automatically. Anything unusual comes back to you.
Automation 03

Process documents

Key details are read, checked and filed for you, ready for a person to confirm.

Trigger
An invoice, purchase order, contract or form arrives by email or lands in a shared folder.
Steps
  1. Read the document and extract the key fields: supplier, number, amount and due date.
  2. Check them against what you expect, such as a matching purchase order.
  3. Write a short summary and save the fields to your sheet or accounting tool.
  4. Queue it for a teammate to check before anything is paid or signed.
Tools

Storage: Google Drive or OneDrive

Reading: ChatGPT or Claude

Records: Google Sheets, Excel, Zoho Books, Xero or QuickBooks

Human approval: the AI prepares and a person checks. Mismatches are flagged, never silently fixed.
Automation 04

Keep teams updated

The right people hear about changes as they happen, so nobody has to chase for an update.

Trigger
A task or order changes status: started, done, delayed or blocked.
Steps
  1. Detect the status change in your task tool or CRM.
  2. Write a one-line summary of what changed and what happens next.
  3. Send it only to the people it concerns: sales, operations or finance.
  4. Post a short daily digest of everything that moved.
Tools

Tasks: Trello, Asana, ClickUp or Notion

Messages: Slack, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp groups or email

Automation: Make, Zapier or n8n

Human approval: you decide who hears about what. Sensitive updates can always wait for a person.
Your first steps

Start this week

Five small steps, no software needed yet. Each one makes the first automation faster to build.

  • Map your enquiries. Write down where they come in today, and who answers them.
  • Choose one home for contacts. A CRM, or even a single Google Sheet.
  • Write three reply templates you would be happy to send to any new lead.
  • Set your follow-up rhythm, for example day 1, day 3 and day 7.
  • Pick the task that interrupts you most, and automate that one first.
