Automation 01

Rule 1 · Don't panic, check

Know in ten minutes which of your AI tools matter most, and that they're still running.

Trigger
Big news about an AI company, a price change, or an outage.
Steps
  1. List every AI tool you use: chatbot, WhatsApp replies, email assistant, CRM notes.
  2. Mark each one: critical, useful or nice-to-have.
  3. Check the vendor's official status page or newsroom, not social media.
  4. Note any announced change to price, terms or availability, and decide if action is needed.
Tools

Inventory: Google Sheets or Notion

Sources: Vendor status pages, official newsrooms

Alerts: Email or WhatsApp to the tool owner

Human approval: you decide what is critical. Nothing changes without your go-ahead.
Automation 02

Rule 2 · No lock-in to one model

If one AI model gets pricier or slower, you swap it in minutes, not months.

Trigger
A model's price, speed, quality or terms change.
Steps
  1. Build agents with a model setting, not a model baked into every step.
  2. Keep two approved models for each workflow, a main one and a backup.
  3. Store prompts outside the tool, so they work with either model.
  4. Test the backup model on real examples before you switch.
Tools

Models: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or open models

Automation: n8n, Make or Zapier, or a custom agent

Testing: A small sheet of sample inputs and expected outputs

Human approval: you approve which models are allowed and when to switch.
Automation 03

Rule 3 · Own your workflow

Your data, prompts and steps stay with you, not stuck inside one tool.

Trigger
You start any new AI workflow, or renew a tool subscription.
Steps
  1. Keep customer data in your own CRM or database, not only inside the AI tool.
  2. Save every prompt and template in a shared document you control.
  3. Write the workflow steps down: trigger, actions, rules, owner.
  4. Check that you can export your data and settings, and test the export once.
Tools

Data: Your CRM, Google Sheets or database

Prompts and steps: Google Docs or Notion

Backups: Scheduled exports to your own drive

Human approval: you control access and exports. Vendors never hold the only copy.
Automation 04

Rule 4 · Human in control

The agent does the work, and you approve what matters.

Trigger
The agent drafts anything that affects money, customers or your brand.
Steps
  1. Decide which actions are automatic (replies within approved templates) and which need you.
  2. Route quotes, discounts, refunds and unusual requests to you for approval.
  3. Approve, edit or reject in one tap from WhatsApp or email.
  4. Log every action, so you can review what the agent did and why.
Tools

Approvals: WhatsApp, Slack or email

Logs: Your CRM or a Google Sheet

Rules: A one-page policy the agent follows

Human approval: anything involving money, commitments or exceptions waits for you.
Your first steps

Start this week

Five small steps, no new software needed.

  • List every AI tool you use and mark the critical ones.
  • Find where each tool keeps your data, and check you can export it.
  • Copy your best prompts into one document you own.
  • Pick one workflow and write its steps on one page.
  • Decide what needs your approval: money, discounts, refunds, exceptions.
